# Asscher assessment: IGI LG836619414

**Preferred visual report:** [[evaluations/IGI-LG836619414-visual-report-2026-10-04-200232.html|Open compact HTML assessment]] — annotated comparisons, frame scrubber, virtual facets and raw-source links. The narrative below is retained as the original detailed assessment.

**Provisional verdict: keep on the shortlist.** This stone shows crisp nested steps, a small defined central pattern and useful changes in contrast around face-up. Its clearest strengths are the legibility of the hall-of-mirrors pattern and a lively outer rim. The principal unresolved question is the relatively pale inner tiers: they remain structured and change with orientation, but these supplier images cannot establish how much useful light they return in ordinary viewing or whether any region leaks. There is no demonstrated optical reject reason in the inspected evidence. This is not a purchase endorsement or a confirmed high-performance cut grade.

## Centre and innermost steps

The centre is not one large undifferentiated black patch. Frames 252–000 show a small dark/light pinwheel-like focal pattern surrounded by trapezoidal inner steps. Across 248, 252, 000 and 008 the central triangular regions change their dark and pale roles, while surrounding inner bands retain a readable structure. This supports central activity within the tested sweep.

The concern is subtler than a dead black centre: several comparatively broad inner panels remain grey/silver over nearby views. They do not look as forceful as the outer dark/bright bands. They may be reflecting diffuse illumination or the camera environment; weak return or leakage cannot be distinguished from those alternatives here. **Confidence: moderate for definition and change, low for efficiency.** The decisive check is small two-axis rocking in neutral illumination, supplemented by correctly associated optical imagery of these exact regions.

## Nested steps / hall of mirrors

This is the strongest supported component. Near-face-up views show distinct horizontal and vertical bands converging towards the centre. Edges remain legible rather than dissolving into a featureless pale table. The upper bands become markedly darker between 248 and 252; neighbouring inner and lower bands do not all follow in the same way. Frames 000–008 retain several separated tiers while the side contrast changes.

That gives a convincing terraced, hall-of-mirrors appearance in this shoot. It does not establish how many physical pavilion steps the stone has or their individual angles. The next check is whether the separation remains attractive under softer everyday illumination. **Confidence: moderate**, supported by multiple nearby frames rather than a single flattering poster.

## Windmills and corners

Four diagonal arms link the central structure towards the clipped corners in the frontal compositions, especially 254–000. The arms are clearly visible but do not dominate the whole table; their apparent width varies by tier. The corner regions contain smaller bright/dark elements, with differences visible as the stone turns through 248–008.

There is no obvious broken or missing arm in these views. Relative brightness changes between corners could arise from lighting and tilt, so unequal brightness in a frame is not a demonstrated cutting defect. Broader versus narrower windmills remains a preference question, not a quality score. **Confidence: moderate for coherent geometry, limited for dynamic balance.** A second rotation direction would test correspondence between opposite corners more usefully.

## Dynamic dance / flash character

The ordered sequence provides actual transition evidence: upper steps darken around 250–254, side bands change across the face-up boundary, and central triangular facets change independently of the entire rim. The stone therefore shows more than one region switching as a single block. Its apparent character combines comparatively broad step-band changes with smaller changes around corners and crown edges.

There is no observed whole-stone simultaneous blackout in the inspected near-face-up interval. Nevertheless, the pale inner tiers look less dramatic than the edge bands. These observations support a promising layered dance in this setup, not a quantified scintillation grade. No real-time flash frequency or human-viewing rhythm can be assigned without the original timing. **Confidence: moderate for spatial switching, low for everyday experience.** Neutral small-rock video is the next useful test.

## Brightness and contrast distribution

The outer frame is consistently prominent, while the centre remains quieter and more silver-grey. Dark bands at the top and sides create an orderly pattern and vary across nearby frames; they do not occupy the whole stone. Some lower-step reflections change colour/brightness while the upper steps remain dark, reinforcing that the contrast is spatially structured.

This is encouraging relative distribution, but the bright supplier setup may make weaker regions appear attractive. The top-versus-bottom and left-versus-right differences are also compatible with an asymmetric reflected environment. Absolute brightness and total returned light cannot be estimated from these JPEGs. **Confidence: moderate for observed distribution, low for efficiency.** Check whether the inner table remains lively under diffuse lighting and at normal viewing distance.

## Leakage / windowing diagnosis

The exact investigation region is the pale inner trapezoidal tiers around the small centre, particularly in 248, 252, 000 and 008. They retain facet boundaries and do show changes, which argues against describing them as a completely inert blank area. It does **not** prove absence of leakage. Neither grey matching the background nor black matching camera obstruction identifies the optical cause by itself.

The strongly oblique frames in the broader rotation are unsuitable for declaring face-up windowing. No region is confirmed as transmitting a known background in useful near-face-up conditions. **Diagnostic confidence: low; leakage remains unknown.** ASET with known backlighting/setup can locate angular return and leakage at that orientation; small-rock video is still needed to assess persistence. Do not reject this stone solely from the pale pixels, or certify leakage control from its brightness.

## Optical symmetry

Around 254–000 the outline and nested central pattern are coherent, with roughly corresponding diagonal structures on all four corners. This is reassuring static appearance. Across the sweep, the two sides do not maintain identical brightness: for example the blue/dark side bands change balance between 252 and 008. Because the stone is turning through non-identical lighting views, this cannot be assigned to a physical or optical asymmetry defect.

Report symmetry Excellent is a factual lab field and does not settle dynamic optical symmetry. **Confidence: moderate for static coherence, limited for dynamic symmetry.** Comparable opposing tilts under symmetric illumination are needed before calling the left/right difference a defect or confirming fully balanced behaviour.

## Crown and periphery

The perimeter stays visually busy around face-up, with smaller contrasting facets at the corners and outer steps. At 248 and 008 there are yellow/blue or other coloured patches around the rim, while broader views 032 and 096 show crown/profile context. These establish visible peripheral activity and coloured reflections in this recording.

They do not isolate dispersion from coloured environmental reflections or image processing; a strong-fire claim would require suitable lighting and motion. The reported 15% crown height is architecture, not proof of that performance. **Confidence: moderate for edge activity, fire unassessed.** A point-source video alongside the diffuse-light recording would test fire without sacrificing the brightness check.

## Overall architecture and presence

The stone presents a near-square clipped outline, a clearly terraced interior and a small central focal pattern. The lively rim frames a quieter inner area. This combination offers a legible geometric Asscher appearance rather than an entirely uniform field of brightness. Visible presence is grounded in its 7.46 mm minimum side, not its 2.55 ct alone.

The main compromise is that the centre's useful light return remains less established than its pattern. A candidate with more vigorous inner-step transitions might be preferable if central drama proves important to the user; another candidate might instead lose some of this stone's clear tier definition. **Confidence: moderate for appearance.** No optical penalty is assigned for depth or extra carat weight.

## Synthesis, value and next checks

**Keep as a provisional candidate.** The best demonstrated features are crisp steps, coherent diagonal structure and changing near-face-up contrast. The pale inner tiers are the highest-value unresolved optical check. Present evidence is sufficient for a feature-level shortlist assessment; it is insufficient to establish leakage control, real-world brightness/fire or a definitive ranking against another stone.

1. Obtain neutral diffuse-light video with small rocking in two directions, concentrating on the inner tiers and whether their bright/dark roles exchange.
2. Seek exact-stone ASET/Ideal-Scope with setup stated. This was searched for previously and not found; no retailer has been contacted.
3. Use point-source motion to assess fire, and seek clarification of the supplier capture-date discrepancy when an authorised opportunity exists.
4. Compare a second candidate side by side on centre activity, tier separation and windmills after the user's size floor is set.
