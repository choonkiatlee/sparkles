# Findings — spatial articulation revisions (#59)

> Provenance note: the pale/quiet-inner annotations referenced below were generated by AI from earlier Sparkles evaluations. They are secondary internal-consistency checks, **not human labels or perceptual ground truth**.

## Result

The two revisions separate cleanly:

- **Candidate 1 — fixed-sector spatial participation: KEEP as a descriptive research primitive.**
- **Candidate 2 — two-sided contrast coverage: REJECT as a standalone descriptor; retain only as an audit challenger.**

Neither result establishes an aesthetic direction or a human-perceptual “good articulation” score.

## Candidate 1: distributed spatial contrast — KEEP

The primary candidate is:

```
distributed_contrast
  = cell_log_spread * contrast_participation
```

where the exact #26 coarse-fixed inner support is partitioned into eight fixed angular sectors, `cell_log_spread` is the robust spread of sector-median log luminance, and `contrast_participation` is the effective fraction of cells participating in that contrast.

### Why it survives

**1. It measures something visibly spatial rather than another global range statistic.**

The automatically selected evidence frames behave as intended. Low values have relatively uniform/locally concentrated inner structure; high values show tonal differences spread across more of the registered inner sectors.

The LG818659722 control is especially useful. Its low frame (source 249) is pale and spatially quiet, while the high/median frames contain broad dark/bright regions across several inner sectors. The descriptor rises strongly because the contrast is spatially distributed. That is real measurement behaviour, even though the grouped darkness is not automatically desirable.

**2. It fixes an important #51 failure mode.**

LG836619414 source 8 has global `Q90-Q10 = 0.524`, yet `distributed_contrast = 0.039`. The large global range is therefore correctly prevented from masquerading as broadly distributed inner structure.

This is exactly the distinction #51 could not make: a frame may contain bright and dark extremes without many parts of the inner architecture participating.

**3. It is materially less redundant in the current four-stone diagnostic.**

Core Q50 Spearman correlations (n=4, descriptive only):

- vs retained inner activation: **0.8**
- vs inner dark occupancy: **0.4**
- vs inner mobility: **0.4**
- vs #51 global raw spread: **0.4**
- vs candidate 2 balanced coverage: **0.4**
- vs #49 centre↔inner contrast: **0.8**
- vs #49 inner↔middle contrast: **0.4**

This is substantially better than #51’s global spread and candidate 2, which largely reproduce existing stone orderings.

**4. The stone ordering is stable to the benchmark window.**

Core-vs-wide Spearman for `distributed_contrast` is **0.8 for Q10, Q50 and Q90**. The absolute values still move with the view window, so the 17-frame core remains the primary comparison; the important result is that the cross-stone ordering is not collapsing when the window widens.

### Core Q50 values

| stone | distributed contrast |
|---|---:|
| LG756520111 | 0.0861 |
| LG756580087 | 0.0632 |
| LG818659722 | 0.2271 |
| LG836619414 | 0.0613 |

LG818659722 is the clear high outlier. This is **not a quality ranking**: its broad grouped-dark structure also distributes contrast across sectors. The descriptor answers “how much spatially distributed inner contrast is present?”, not “is that contrast attractive?”.

### What it still does not solve

The earlier AI pale-inner annotations are not perfectly ordered by this statistic, especially for LG756520111. That is not grounds to tune the metric to those labels: the labels are AI-generated and the source evidence shows that “distributed contrast” and “pale/quiet appearance” are not guaranteed to be the same percept.

The correct retained interpretation is therefore narrow:

> **distributed inner contrast / participation across coarse spatial sectors**

Do not rename it “articulation quality” or infer that higher is better.

## Candidate 2: balanced contrast coverage — REJECT standalone

The primary candidate was:

```
balanced_coverage = min(p_dark, p_bright)
```

with dark/bright defined relative to the within-inner median using a fixed 15% ratio, plus 10% and 20% sensitivity checks.

The source panels do show what the metric claims to measure: frames with more area on both sides of the median tend to receive larger values. So the primitive itself is valid.

It does not survive the incremental-information bar.

### Window instability

Core-vs-wide Q50 Spearman:

- 10% threshold: **0.4**
- 15% primary threshold: **0.0**
- 20% threshold: **-0.21**

By comparison, candidate 1 is 0.8 at Q50.

The Q10/Q90 orderings are often more stable, but the natural stone-level median summary is both window-sensitive and threshold-sensitive.

### Redundancy

At the primary 15% threshold, core Q50 has Spearman **1.0** versus each of:

- retained inner occupancy;
- retained inner mobility;
- #51 global raw spread;
- #49 inner↔middle contrast.

With n=4 this is only a diagnostic, but together with the window/threshold instability it gives no reason to retain another standalone scalar.

### Disposition

Reject `balanced_coverage_1p15` as an independent descriptor. Keep the dark/bright fractions and fixed threshold sweep available inside the #59 audit output because they remain useful for explaining why a frame receives a particular spatial-participation value.

Do not promote them into the retained descriptor profile.

## Relationship to the earlier AI annotations

The strongest useful consistency example is LG836619414:

- source 248: distributed contrast 0.0285; global spread 0.2297
- source 0: distributed contrast 0.0360; global spread 0.2818
- source 8: distributed contrast 0.0390; global spread 0.5235

The earlier AI evaluation called these inner views pale/quiet. #51’s global spread changes dramatically at source 8, while candidate 1 remains consistently low. This is encouraging internal consistency, **not human validation**.

LG756520111 is a counterexample to treating the AI annotation as a clean numeric target: its labelled frames span low to high distributed contrast. Preserve that mismatch rather than fitting it away.

## Diagnostic pairs for future human labelling

The fixed-2%-brightness evidence selector provides a compact first human-review set for candidate 1:

- LG756520111: sources **253 vs 254**
- LG756580087: **253 vs 4**
- LG818659722: **3 vs 5**
- LG836619414: **0 vs 6**

These pairs have closely matched whole-stone brightness but materially different distributed contrast. A future blinded comparison can ask which frame has clearer/richer inner spatial articulation without exposing the metric values.

That would test a genuine human percept rather than recursively calibrating AI evaluation language against AI-derived measurements.

## Next direction

Do **not** proceed automatically to another metric.

Candidate 1 already supplies the missing coarse spatial primitive. Candidate 3 (Asscher-specific panel-to-panel articulation) is worth testing only if a small human-labelled pair set shows that the fixed eight-sector representation misses a repeatable perceptual distinction.

Hold candidate 4 (temporal spatial participation) until there is evidence that static spatial participation is insufficient.

## Final interpretation

The useful decomposition is now:

- #51 global spread: **how wide is the inner tonal range?** — audit only;
- #59 candidate 1: **how broadly is inner contrast distributed spatially?** — KEEP descriptive primitive;
- #59 candidate 2: **how much area lies on both sides of a threshold?** — REJECT standalone / audit only.

This is a cleaner measurement vocabulary than trying to collapse all three into one “articulation” score.
